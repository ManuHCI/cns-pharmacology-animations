Three States of Na⁺ Channel
1. RESTING (Closed but available) m-gate (activation gate) CLOSED · h-gate (inactivation gate) OPEN. Channel is capable of opening if depolarized.
2. OPEN (Activated) — ~1 ms Depolarization → m-gate opens. Na⁺ rushes in down its gradient. Rising phase of action potential.
3. INACTIVATED (Closed, unavailable) h-gate ("ball & chain") swings shut. Channel cannot reopen regardless of voltage. This is the absolute refractory period.
+ DRUG BOUND (Use-dependent block) Phenytoin / Carbamazepine bind with high affinity to the INACTIVATED state. The drug stabilises this state → recovery is delayed → channel stays unavailable longer.
Why "use-dependent"? Drugs bind preferentially when channels are repeatedly opening & inactivating (as in a seizure focus). Normal neurons firing at low frequency are largely spared — this is why these drugs suppress seizures without abolishing normal CNS activity.
Clinically Important Drugs
  • Phenytoin — partial & GTC seizures; status epilepticus (IV)
  • Carbamazepine — partial seizures (DOC); trigeminal neuralgia
  • Oxcarbazepine — better-tolerated CBZ analogue
  • Lamotrigine — broad spectrum; also absence & LGS
  • Lacosamide — enhances slow inactivation
  • Zonisamide, Topiramate — multiple mechanisms incl. Na⁺ block
